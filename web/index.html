“Year 10 maths is getting difficult.”For a parent · An example, not a diagnosis
  1. 1
    Start with one question
    “Where did this answer go wrong?”
    Talk with your child. Give your AI the question and their working, without names or school details.
  2. 2
    Trace the foundations
    Pythagoras → squares → square roots
    Ask AI to check each idea. Can they identify the hypotenuse? Do they think √25 is 12.5? Let their answers guide the next check.
  3. 3
    Practise the missing step
    Try a short exercise, then return to the original question.
    One mistake is a clue. Check understanding before deciding what needs more practice.
What does “below expectations” mean in NZ?

For Years 0–10, reports use progress descriptors: Emerging, Developing, Consolidating, Proficient and Exceeding. Their meaning depends on the point in the school year. For an NCEA standard, a result below the criteria is called Not Achieved.

About the NZ version

A learning map your AI can read.

1,870 topics and 4,004 connections help your AI find the small steps behind a bigger learning goal.

How it works

Connected learning steps

The AI can search topics, check prerequisites and build a practice sequence. Each topic includes examples and questions to support learning at home.

NZ connections

Learning through everyday life

The original Marble graph is joined by 16 NZ activities and a maths supplement spanning Years 0–13. It adds 264 topics and enriches 152 existing topics, with diagnostic questions, hints and separate curriculum mappings. Research drafts and remaining coverage gaps are documented.

Open data

Free to use and build on

The graph and source code are open. Explore the data, suggest a correction or connect your own learning tool.

View the project

Privacy & use

Keep learning personal.

No account needed

The public MCP reads the learning graph. It does not create child profiles or save your learning questions. Topic checkmarks stay in your browser. The hosting provider processes requests and technical logs to run the service.

You stay in the conversation

Use your own AI account and review its suggestions with your child. Your AI provider handles conversations under its own terms. You can describe a learning need without sharing a child's name or school.
