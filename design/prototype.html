        Fractions of a whole

        Mathematics · Fractions Ages 8–9 · about Year 4–5 Ready next for Aroha

        Knowing that a fraction is a number of equal parts. One quarter is one piece of something cut into four equal pieces, and three quarters is three of those pieces.

        How you will know they have got it

        Tick what you have seen

        0 of 3 seen so far. In a parent account, this is saved to your child’s profile.

        A question to ask

        Casual is best: in the car, over dinner
        “If we cut this pizza into 6 equal slices, what does one sixth mean? What would four sixths look like?” From the step’s check-in question, reworded for home

        Try it at home, NZ style

        Local example · draft

        Cutting the pav for the whānau

        Next time there is a pavlova (or a quiche, or a pizza) on the table, let your child do the cutting.

        1. Ask them to cut it into 6 equal slices. Talk about why “equal” matters.
        2. Each person who takes a slice, ask: “What fraction is gone now?”
        3. At the end: “Is 6/6 the same as one whole pav?”

        Words you might hear at school: hautoru (a third), hauwhā (a quarter), haurua (a half). Reo Māori terms are listed for interest and still need checking by a fluent speaker.

        About the NZ version

        Useful, open, and honest about being a draft.

        The NZ adaptation is a draft built from published, openly licensed sources. It is not endorsed by the Ministry of Education and has not yet been reviewed by NZ teachers.

        Where it comes from

        Published curriculum documents

        Each step links back to the source statement it came from, such as the England national curriculum or international frameworks. Every source keeps its own licence, listed in full on our provenance page.

        What we have changed

        NZ English, Year levels, local examples

        Ages are shown with approximate NZ Year levels. Examples use everyday Aotearoa life. Links to the NZ Curriculum are suggestions made from public documents.

        What it is not

        Not official, not an assessment

        It is not a Ministry resource, a school report, or a test. Children learn at different paces, and a step being “later” is never a problem.
